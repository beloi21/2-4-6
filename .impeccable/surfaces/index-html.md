Scope: the single game screen of 2-4-6 (index.html). Mode: Experience. Build path: code-led, because the author pinned live real-time 3D, which a measured image comp cannot govern.

Audience and job: two people share one device and play misère Nim, 2-4-6, tapping bricks and teasing the loser.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A dusk brickyard, not a game UI. You handle actual fired clay bricks on packed ground while the score is chalked on a soot wall. Refuses the pastel rounded casual-puzzle screen with cards and pill buttons.

OWN-WORLD: Soot wall #17130F, fired clay #A8492C with mottling and a stamped maker's mark, chalk #EFE7D8 for every piece of text, ember #E8935A only for the chosen pile. One low warm sun raking from the right throws shadows across the stacks and the ground. Type: Cabin Sketch display, Patrick Hand text. Controls are chalk: filled block for the primary, outline for the secondary. No cards, gradients or glass.

STORY: Players grasp the rule from the stacks and one chalked line, tap bricks, watch the total, press Remove, pass the device. The loser gets mocked by the last brick, and they immediately play again.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Top: title and chalked names with tally marks. Middle, largest: three real-time 3D stacks of 2, 4 and 6 bricks standing left to right, with remaining counts chalked on the ground. Bottom: Reset outline left, large Remove block right.

Signature interaction: tapped bricks slide out of the stack towards you under a chalk mark; Remove hurls them, bricks above drop with a thud and dust. The last brick grows, lands centre stage, sprouts googly eyes and blows a raspberry at the loser.

Motion grammar: weight and gravity, every drop lands with a thud. Reduced motion places things directly.

FORM: Brickyard at Dusk, grounded list position 1 (kiln yard), chosen by the author over the rolled direction. seed:5defadcb6de589eb
